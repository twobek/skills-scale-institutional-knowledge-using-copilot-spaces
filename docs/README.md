## OctoAcme Project Management Approach

OctoAcme follows a customer-centric, iterative approach to project management built on five core principles: **Customer-first** (prioritize customer value and usability), **Iterative delivery** (deliver small, testable increments), **Clear ownership** (each project has a named Project Manager and Product Lead), **Data-informed decisions** (measure impact and iterate based on evidence), and **Psychological safety** (encourage feedback and learning).

### Lifecycle & Workflow Structure

OctoAcme follows a disciplined five-stage project lifecycle: **Initiation**, **Planning**, **Execution**, **Release**, and **Close & Retrospective**. The organization emphasizes iterative delivery through sprint-based execution with clear milestones and decision gates. Projects begin with lightweight validation—a one-pager documenting the problem statement, success metrics, and stakeholder alignment—before moving into detailed planning. Once approved, teams create prioritized backlogs with acceptance criteria, estimate work using T-shirt sizing or story points, and organize execution around daily standups, weekly delivery syncs, and sprint-based iterations. This structured approach balances flexibility with accountability, ensuring alignment across teams from initiation through retrospective.

### Personas & Roles

OctoAcme operates with three core roles that share complementary responsibilities. **Project Managers** coordinate delivery activities, manage schedules, risks, and stakeholder communications to ensure on-time, in-scope delivery. **Product Managers** define the "what" and "why"—owning vision, prioritizing the backlog, and measuring outcomes through data-driven success metrics. **Developers** implement features, collaborate on design and testability, and contribute to planning and risk identification. This clear role separation enables ownership and accountability while fostering cross-functional collaboration through weekly PM-Product alignment, regular standup participation, and structured code reviews.

### Communication & Risk Management

Communication at OctoAcme is systematic and multi-layered: weekly syncs between PM and Product Manager, twice-weekly standups for delivery teams, monthly stakeholder updates, and ad-hoc escalations as needed. Risk management is embedded throughout the project lifecycle—teams maintain a simple risk register tracking impact, likelihood, mitigation plans, and status, reviewed weekly. Escalation follows a clear path (team → PM → Product Lead → Sponsor), with distinct playbooks for standard updates and incident communication. This ensures transparency, rapid problem-solving, and alignment across organizational levels.

### Quality Assurance & Continuous Improvement

Quality is built into execution through multiple checkpoints: automated testing and linting in CI before PR approval, unit and integration tests for new logic, end-to-end smoke tests before release, and manual QA when needed. Release management is standardized with pre-release requirements (acceptance criteria met, security scans passed, rollback plans documented) and a clear deployment checklist. After each sprint, release, or milestone, retrospectives capture learnings—identifying what went well and what can improve—with action items added to the backlog and tracked through weekly syncs. This continuous improvement cycle, combined with metrics tracking and velocity monitoring, ensures OctoAcme sustains quality while iteratively refining its processes.

## Quick Reference Guide

### I'm new to OctoAcme. Where do I start?
Start with [Project Management Overview](octoacme-project-management-overview.md) for a concise introduction to our approach, then review [Roles and Personas](octoacme-roles-and-personas.md) to understand your role and responsibilities.

### I'm starting a new project. What should I do?
Follow the process outlined in [Project Initiation](octoacme-project-initiation.md) to validate your idea, create a one-pager, and align stakeholders. Once approved, move to [Project Planning](octoacme-project-planning.md) to build your detailed plan and backlog.

### I'm managing day-to-day delivery. What guidance should I use?
Refer to [Execution and Tracking](octoacme-execution-and-tracking.md) for workflows, team rhythm, quality practices, and metrics. Use [Risks and Communication](octoacme-risks-and-communication.md) to manage blockers and keep stakeholders informed.

### We're preparing a release. What's the process?
Review [Release and Deployment](octoacme-release-and-deployment.md) for pre-release requirements, deployment checklist, rollback procedures, and release notes templates.

### Our sprint/project is ending. What happens next?
Conduct a retrospective following the guidance in [Retrospective and Continuous Improvement](octoacme-retrospective-and-continuous-improvement.md) to capture learnings and define action items for the next cycle.

### I need to identify and manage project risks.
Use the risk management framework in [Risks and Communication](octoacme-risks-and-communication.md), which includes risk register templates, lifecycle guidance, and escalation procedures.

## Document Maintenance

These documents are living artifacts and should be updated when processes change or new best practices emerge. Please follow this workflow:

1. **Propose changes** — Open an issue describing what should be updated and why
2. **Review with stakeholders** — Get alignment from your PM, Product Manager, or team lead
3. **Update the document** — Create a pull request with your changes
4. **Communicate broadly** — Notify the team and highlight significant changes in standups or team meetings

If you have questions about any process, encounter gaps in the documentation, or have suggestions for improvement, please:
- Open an issue in the repository
- Reach out to your Project Manager or Product Manager
- Bring topics to your retrospectives and continuous improvement discussions
